# Acceptable Use Policy — Umbra

**Status:** Published for transparency · Effective **2026-08-16**  
**Related:** [Terms](/legal/terms) · [Privacy](/legal/privacy) · site Guide

## 1. Lawful use only

You may use the service only for **lawful** purposes. You must not use Umbra to
violate applicable criminal or civil law (including computer misuse, fraud,
sanctions, child sexual abuse material offenses, illegal stalking/harassment
statutes, and trade in stolen data where prohibited).

## 2. Authorization

You must only investigate systems, people, or data when you have a **lawful
basis** to do so. Where the product records an authorization basis, it must be
accurate.

## 3. Service abuse

Prohibited:

- Circumventing rate limits or technical protections  
- Attacking, scanning, or overloading Umbra infrastructure except authorized
  security research disclosed under SECURITY.md  
- Using the Service as an open proxy or bulk scanner against third parties
  without authorization  
- Attempting to access the operator console or other users' cases  

## 4. Content

You must not submit or process content that is illegal to possess or transmit
(including CSAM). We may remove data and report to authorities when required by
law.

## 5. Dual-use OSINT

Passive collection of publicly available information may be lawful in many
contexts and is not banned solely for being “intrusive.” **Illegality**, not
discomfort, is the line. You remain responsible for source terms of service and
local law.

## 6. Findings are not consumer reports

Do not use Umbra outputs to make decisions about credit, insurance, employment,
housing, or other purposes regulated by the FCRA or similar laws.

## 7. Enforcement

We may rate-limit, block, delete data, and cooperate with law enforcement when
we reasonably believe the service is used for illegal activity or severe abuse.

## 8. Changes

We may update this AUP; the effective date will change. Continued use after
updates constitutes acceptance where permitted by law.

## Phone number pages

`/phone` exists so someone can check an unknown caller. It is not a tool for
finding out who a number belongs to, and we do not provide that.

You may not use it to:

- harass, stalk, intimidate or "expose" a person;
- research a specific individual's phone line for personal disputes;
- report a number to punish, embarrass or pressure whoever answers it;
- scrape or bulk-collect the numbers or reports.

Community reports, once open, are **allegations by strangers** — not findings by
Umbra, not evidence of a crime, and not a claim about anybody's identity.
Repeated false or retaliatory reports lose access.
